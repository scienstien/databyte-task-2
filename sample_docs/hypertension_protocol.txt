HYPERTENSION MANAGEMENT PROTOCOL
(Sample Document for Testing)

1. DEFINITION AND CLASSIFICATION
Hypertension is defined as a sustained systolic blood pressure (SBP) >= 140 mmHg and/or diastolic blood pressure (DBP) >= 90 mmHg, measured on at least two separate occasions.

Classification (ESC/ESH 2023 Guidelines):
- Optimal: SBP < 120 and DBP < 80 mmHg
- Normal: SBP 120–129 and/or DBP 80–84 mmHg
- High-normal: SBP 130–139 and/or DBP 85–89 mmHg
- Grade 1 Hypertension: SBP 140–159 and/or DBP 90–99 mmHg
- Grade 2 Hypertension: SBP 160–179 and/or DBP 100–109 mmHg
- Grade 3 Hypertension: SBP >= 180 and/or DBP >= 110 mmHg
- Isolated Systolic Hypertension: SBP >= 140 and DBP < 90 mmHg

2. MEASUREMENT
Blood pressure should be measured after 5 minutes of rest in a seated position. At least two readings 1–2 minutes apart should be averaged. Confirm hypertension with ambulatory blood pressure monitoring (ABPM) or home blood pressure monitoring (HBPM) wherever possible.

White coat hypertension is defined as office BP >= 140/90 mmHg but 24-hour ABPM or HBPM < 130/80 mmHg. Masked hypertension is the reverse pattern.

3. TREATMENT TARGETS
- General adult population: < 130/80 mmHg (if tolerated)
- Age >= 65 years: SBP 130–139 mmHg (individualized, avoid < 120 mmHg)
- Diabetes: < 130/80 mmHg
- Chronic kidney disease: < 130/80 mmHg
- Post-stroke: < 130/80 mmHg

4. NON-PHARMACOLOGICAL INTERVENTIONS
All patients with hypertension or high-normal BP should be advised on lifestyle modifications:
- Sodium reduction: < 5 g/day dietary salt intake
- DASH diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension): rich in fruits, vegetables, low-fat dairy, and low in saturated fat
- Weight reduction: Target BMI 18.5–24.9 kg/m²; each 10 kg weight loss reduces SBP by approximately 6 mmHg
- Physical activity: 150–300 minutes/week moderate aerobic activity, or 75 minutes vigorous activity
- Alcohol limitation: < 14 units/week for men, < 8 units/week for women
- Smoking cessation: strongly recommended (does not directly lower BP but greatly reduces cardiovascular risk)

5. PHARMACOLOGICAL MANAGEMENT

5.1 First-line agents
Four major drug classes are recommended as first-line therapy:
a) ACE inhibitors (ACEi) — e.g. ramipril, lisinopril, perindopril
b) Angiotensin receptor blockers (ARBs) — e.g. losartan, valsartan, irbesartan
c) Calcium channel blockers (CCBs) — e.g. amlodipine, felodipine
d) Thiazide/thiazide-like diuretics — e.g. indapamide, chlorthalidone

ACEi and ARBs should NOT be combined together due to increased risk of hyperkalaemia, renal dysfunction, and hypotension.

5.2 Preferred agents in specific conditions
- Heart failure with reduced EF: ACEi + beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT-2i
- Post-myocardial infarction: ACEi + beta-blocker
- Stable angina: CCB or beta-blocker
- Atrial fibrillation: ACEi or ARB (+ beta-blocker for rate control)
- Chronic kidney disease with proteinuria: ACEi or ARB (renally protective)
- Pregnancy: methyldopa, labetalol, or nifedipine (avoid ACEi/ARB — teratogenic)
- Black African patients: CCB or thiazide diuretic preferred over ACEi/ARB monotherapy

5.3 Combination therapy
Most patients require 2 or more drugs to achieve target BP. Preferred combination is:
ACEi or ARB + CCB; if insufficient, add thiazide diuretic (triple therapy).
Resistant hypertension (uncontrolled on 3 drugs including a diuretic): add spironolactone 25–50 mg/day.

6. FOLLOW-UP
After initiating or changing therapy, review in 4 weeks. Once stable, annual review is acceptable. Monitor renal function, electrolytes, and BP at each visit. Consider ABPM annually in treated patients to exclude white coat effect.

7. HYPERTENSIVE EMERGENCIES
Hypertensive emergency: severely elevated BP (usually > 180/120 mmHg) with evidence of acute target organ damage (encephalopathy, AKI, pulmonary oedema, aortic dissection, STEMI/NSTEMI). Requires IV therapy — labetalol, nicardipine, or sodium nitroprusside depending on the scenario. Reduce mean arterial pressure by no more than 25% in the first hour.

Hypertensive urgency: severely elevated BP without organ damage. Oral therapy; aim to lower BP over 24–48 hours.
